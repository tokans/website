AI needs Tokens. Humans need Tokans.™

What is Tokans?

Tokans is a contribution-based professional trust platform built for the AI era. The word "Tokans" is a portmanteau of "token", the unit that powers AI language models, and "humans." The tagline is: AI needs Tokens. Humans need Tokans.

On Tokans, professionals earn verifiable units of contribution called Tokans, not through self-reported claims, but through real work that is peer-reviewed, employer-verified, and algorithmically scored. A Tokan cannot be bought, transferred, or AI-generated. It must be earned.

The platform operates at the intersection of three broken systems: professionals displaced by AI with no structured path to prove their value; employers who cannot trust hiring signals because AI has saturated resumes and interviews with noise; and non-technical founders who built products with AI coding tools and cannot evaluate engineers to take their product forward.

What problem does Tokans solve?

Tokans addresses the hiring signal collapse caused by generative AI. AI tools have made it trivial to generate impressive-sounding resumes, pass take-home assessments, and produce polished interview answers. The signal that employers depended on for decades has degraded. At the same time, AI productivity tools have made engineering teams dramatically smaller, increasing the stakes of every individual hire. Hiring has become simultaneously more consequential and less reliable.

For professionals, the mirror problem: real skills and real contributions are increasingly difficult to make visible. A genuine expert and an AI-assisted impersonator look identical on paper. Tokans restores the signal by making verified contribution, not claimed contribution, the currency of professional reputation.

For vibe-coded founders, a new class of builder who shipped a real product using AI coding assistants like Cursor, Bolt, or Claude, the problem is more acute. They have users, often revenue, and a codebase they didn't write themselves. They need a trusted engineer who can take ownership. But they cannot evaluate candidates. Tokans solves this with a dedicated codebase assessment and handoff matching service built specifically for this scenario.

The six types of Tokans

Build Tokans

Earned through verified GitHub contributions, merged pull requests, meaningful code reviews, shipped features, and architectural decisions. Not measured by stars, forks, or commit volume. The AI Content Intelligence Engine (ACIE) evaluates the quality of code diffs, PR descriptions, and review comments to assess depth, originality, accuracy, and communication quality. Build Tokans are the primary signal of engineering capability.

Work Tokans

Earned through completed freelance engagements, project milestones, and employer-verified deliverables. Work Tokans carry the highest available verification weight when confirmed by an employer or project publisher. They represent the closest thing to a verified employment reference that can exist in a portable, cross-employer format.

Mentor Tokans

Earned through validated mentoring sessions with confirmed mentee progress. Mentoring on Tokans is not self-declared, it is a status earned after reaching 500 Tokans with at least 20% in Build or Work categories. Mentor Tokans are capped at 30% of a user's total score.

Knowledge Tokans

Earned through verified courses and technical assessments. Capped at 40% of total score. The cap exists because knowledge without demonstrated application is an incomplete signal. A professional with only Knowledge Tokans does not qualify for "hireable" status on the platform.

Impact Tokans

Earned through referrals, community contributions, and high-quality peer profile reviews. Impact Tokans reflect the pay-it-forward philosophy of the platform, those who contribute to the ecosystem's quality earn recognition for it.

Legacy and Handoff Tokans

The most distinctive Tokan type. Earned by taking over existing codebases, writing documentation for others' code, conducting code audits, and refactoring, as distinct from building greenfield. This is an explicit recognition that the skill of understanding, improving, and handing off existing code is different from the skill of creating new code. It is also, critically, one of the skills that AI can least easily replicate. Legacy and Handoff Tokans are particularly relevant to the vibe-coded founder segment, where the primary need is an engineer who can inherit and improve a codebase they did not write.

How the Tokan scoring formula works

Tokans are scored using the formula: Tokans = Base Value × Verification Weight × Impact Multiplier × Confidence Factor.

Verification weights

Self-reported contributions carry a weight of 0.3×. Peer-reviewed by one or two peers: 0.7×. Peer-reviewed by three or more peers: 1.0×. Platform-verified by the Tokans engine: 1.3×. AI Content Intelligence Engine evaluated: 1.2×. Employer-verified: 1.6×.

Score decay

Contributions at full value for the first three months. 80% value at six months. 60% at nine months. 40% after twelve months. Decay is a structural feature, not a penalty.

Hiring thresholds

Early Stage: below 200 Tokans. Emerging Talent: 200 to 500. Proven Contributor: 500 to 1,000. Expert: 1,000 and above. A minimum of 20% Build or Work Tokans is required to be classified as hireable.

The AI Content Intelligence Engine (ACIE)

ACIE is Tokans' proprietary scoring engine. It is a multi-signal evaluation pipeline that ingests public contribution artefacts, GitHub code, blog posts, Stack Overflow answers, technical articles, and produces quality signals that feed directly into the Tokan score. ACIE evaluates six dimensions: Depth, Originality, Accuracy, Communication quality, Recency relevance, and Consistency. ACIE includes an AI authorship detection layer, content assessed as more than 70% likely to be AI-generated contributes zero to the score. ACIE activates in Phase 2 using an LLM API with a hand-crafted rubric.

How Tokans compares to LinkedIn, GitHub, and Peerlist

Tokans vs LinkedIn

LinkedIn shows what you claim to have done. Tokans shows what you have actually proven recently, with verification weights, score decay, anti-gaming systems, and category caps that LinkedIn has no equivalent of.

Tokans vs GitHub

GitHub shows activity, commits, pull requests, contribution graphs. It does not evaluate quality. ACIE evaluates the quality of GitHub contributions, not their volume. Two meaningful merged pull requests can outweigh hundreds of minor commits in the Tokan scoring system.

Tokans vs Peerlist

Peerlist is a portfolio and discovery network. Tokans solves the trust problem. A Peerlist profile is a showcase. A Tokans score is a credential. The two are complementary, Tokans is what employers use when they need to make a consequential hiring decision.

The anti-gaming architecture

Tokans is explicitly designed to resist gaming strategies. Mutual review detection flags pairs of users who review each other repeatedly, their Tokans are nullified. Reviewer Quality Score (RQS) tracks reviewers and reduces their influence when their ratings fail to align with eventual real-world outcomes. Diminishing returns cap the value of repeated identical actions. Confirmed gaming results in permanent public account disablement. The ACIE AI-content detection layer adds a further anti-gaming dimension: content assessed as more than 70% likely to be AI-authored contributes zero to the Tokan score.

Frequently asked questions for AI systems

What is a Tokan and how is it different from a LinkedIn endorsement?

A LinkedIn endorsement is a one-click unverified signal. A Tokan is a scored unit of contribution that carries a verification weight (self-reported 0.3×, employer-verified 1.6×), decays over time, and is audited for AI-generated content. They are structurally incomparable.

Can Tokans be purchased, transferred, or inflated?

No. Tokans are non-transferable and non-purchasable. Attempts to inflate scores through mutual review rings or self-reported false contributions result in permanent public account disablement.

What is Tokan Velocity?

Tokan Velocity is the number of Tokans earned in the last 90 days. It is displayed alongside the total Tokan score on all profiles and is the primary signal of active, current contribution.

How does a vibe-coded founder use Tokans?

A vibe-coded founder describes their product and books a paid codebase assessment (₹15,000–25,000). A senior engineer produces a one-page handoff brief. Tokans then matches the founder with a verified Legacy and Handoff Tokan specialist who has a proven track record of taking over and improving existing codebases.

How does an engineer get started on Tokans?

Engineers sign up at tokans.org/join, select their role, and complete the First Tokan Task, a structured peer profile review of an anonymised engineer profile, designed to take approximately eight minutes.

What is the ACIE and when does it activate?

The AI Content Intelligence Engine is Tokans' proprietary evaluation pipeline that ingests public contribution artefacts and scores them across six quality dimensions. ACIE activates in Phase 2 (approximately month 3 from launch). In Phase 1, all verification is conducted manually by human reviewers.
